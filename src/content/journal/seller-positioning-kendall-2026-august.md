Positioning a Kendall single-family home before pricing it: the neighborhood, not the wider area, and what buyers verify first.

## What Kendall Buyers Are Actually Comparing

Kendall is not one market. The difference between neighborhoods a few streets apart is real, and buyers know it.

Multigenerational buyers place real value on space that other buyers discount. How the floor plan is described changes which of them shows up. The likely buyer here is drawn from Miami family, multigenerational, and local move-up buyers, and the property is being read against a large suburban market where neighborhoods a few blocks apart trade on different terms. A list price set without knowing which comparison the property will lose is a guess wearing a number.

## The Diligence That Decides a Kendall Deal

The items that most often stall a sale here:

- Permit history for additions, enclosed spaces, and in-law conversions
- Association rules where the home sits inside one
- Roof, HVAC, and plumbing age

None of them improve by waiting, and each is cheaper to resolve on the seller's own schedule than under a contract deadline with a buyer holding the calendar. The specific failure to plan around is unpermitted enclosed space counted as living area in the listing and removed by the appraiser.

## Where the Price Actually Comes From

Pricing should be built from the specific neighborhood rather than the wider area, lot size and genuinely usable outdoor space, and whether additions were permitted and count as living area — not from a per-square-foot figure applied across the city. Overpricing does not create negotiating room. It removes the property from the searches of the buyers most likely to pay full value, and the correction costs more than the original discipline would have.
