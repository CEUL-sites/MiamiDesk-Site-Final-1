Florida voters approved Amendment 3 on November 3, 2026. Effective January 1, 2027, the measure lowers the annual assessment-growth limit for qualifying non-homestead property from 10% to 5%. It also expands the homestead exemption for eligible permanent residents. The changes do not affect 2026 property tax bills. [Leon County Property Appraiser](https://www.leonpa.gov/Amendment3)

For South Florida rental owners, multifamily investors and second-home buyers, the key distinction is straightforward: **a 5% assessment cap is not a 5% cap on the total tax bill.**

The investment question is how the lower limit interacts with an acquisition, existing assessment benefits, improvements and local tax rates.

## What the 5% non-homestead assessment cap actually changes

Beginning in 2027, the lower limit applies to annual increases in assessed value for eligible non-homestead property, including rental homes, second homes and qualifying commercial property. It applies to **non-school levies**. School taxes remain outside this assessment limitation. [Seminole County Property Appraiser](https://www.scpafl.org/information/property-tax-initiative)

The potential benefit is slower growth in part of the property's tax base over an extended holding period. It does not guarantee lower taxes, stable net operating income or a particular refinancing outcome. Millage rates, school taxes and non-ad valorem assessments still affect the amount payable. [Leon County Property Appraiser](https://www.leonpa.gov/Amendment3)

### How the difference can compound

Consider an illustrative property with a starting non-school assessed value of $1 million:

- **Starting assessment:** $1,000,000 under either limit.
- **After one annual increase:** $1,050,000 at 5%; $1,100,000 at the former 10%.
- **After five annual increases:** $1,276,282 at 5%; $1,610,510 at the former 10%.

*Author calculations: $1,000,000 × 1.05ⁿ and $1,000,000 × 1.10ⁿ, rounded to the nearest dollar. This comparison assumes just value supports every maximum increase, uninterrupted eligibility, and no ownership reset or improvement adjustment. The 10% scenario is a hypothetical comparison with the former limit, not a second option available to owners.*

The difference after five increases is approximately **$334,228 in assessed value**. That is not a dollar amount of tax savings. Actual taxes require the applicable taxable values and millage rates.

## The acquisition risk: inheriting the seller's tax assumptions

An existing owner's capped assessment can materially understate a buyer's future tax base.

Under Florida's existing reassessment rules, a qualifying change of ownership or control generally resets a non-homestead property's assessment to just value on January 1 of the following year, subject to statutory exceptions. Buying interests in the property-owning entity can also trigger reassessment: the rules address cumulative transfers of control or more than 50% of entity ownership. A recorded deed is not the only possible trigger. [Florida Statutes §193.1554](https://wwwo.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.1554.html) and [§193.1555](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/193.1555)

For underwriting, estimate the **first tax year following acquisition**, then model later years separately. Use the county property appraiser's buyer estimator where available, the anticipated post-transfer just value, parcel-specific millages and applicable assessments. Purchase price can inform the estimate; it is not an automatic determination of assessed value.

For a December 2026 purchase, the relevant January 1 follows almost immediately. For a 2027 purchase, it falls in 2028. A generic “Year 2” label can obscure that timing.

Owners must promptly notify the property appraiser of qualifying ownership or control changes; recording the relevant deed or instrument serves as notice. Failure to report can expose improperly retained benefits to back taxes, interest, penalties and liens. [Florida Statutes §193.1556](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/193.1556)

## Renovations can change more than the improvement's assessment

Florida law distinguishes property categories. For non-homestead residential property with nine or fewer units, additions and improvements generally enter the assessment at just value on the first January 1 after substantial completion, subject to exceptions. [Florida Statutes §193.1554](https://wwwo.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.1554.html)

For property assessed under §193.1555, including many larger multifamily and commercial assets, a qualifying improvement that increases the property's just value by **at least 25%** can trigger reassessment of the property itself to just value the following January 1. Repairs, replacements and specified reconstruction circumstances receive different treatment. [Florida Statutes §193.1555](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/193.1555)

Value-add investors should have their renovation scope reviewed before assuming the existing assessment benefit will survive the project.

## Homestead changes: relevant to relocation, not every investment purchase

For qualifying homesteads, the new non-school exemption is up to **$150,000 in 2027** and **$250,000 in 2028**, with positive inflation adjustments beginning in 2029. The school-tax exemption remains up to $25,000. These are exemptions from assessed value, not tax credits. [Enrolled CS/HJR 1F, Article VII, Section 6](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)

Owners who were not permanent Florida residents by December 31, 2026, face a different initial structure: up to $50,000 for non-school taxes, adjusted for inflation beginning in 2028. The larger exemption begins with the **fifth year of homestead exemption**, subject to constitutional and implementation requirements. Simply spending five calendar years in Florida is not the stated test. [Seminole County Property Appraiser](https://www.scpafl.org/information/property-tax-initiative)

Purchasing by December 31 does not, by itself, establish permanent residency or homestead eligibility. Owners planning a move should confirm their circumstances with the property appraiser and counsel. An investment or second-home purchase does not automatically qualify for homestead benefits.

## Local rates and assessments still matter

The revised millage voting rules come from **separate legislation, Chapter 2026-240**, rather than Amendment 3 itself. That law took effect June 24, 2026. It generally uses a rolled-back-rate framework, with a two-thirds vote for certain increases up to 110% of the applicable baseline. Higher rates can require unanimity, a three-fourths vote for governing bodies with nine or more members, or a referendum. Additional exceptions apply. [Chapter 2026-240, Sections 2 and 9](https://laws.flrules.org/2026/240)

Do not assume either automatic rate increases or permanently frozen rates. Underwrite the property's actual taxing districts and review non-ad valorem charges separately. Forecasting tax savings before future rates and implementation details are settled would be premature. [Leon County Property Appraiser](https://www.leonpa.gov/Amendment3)

## What South Florida investors should do now

1. **Separate the tax components.** Identify just value, capped assessed value, exemptions, school taxes and non-ad valorem assessments for each holding.
2. **Rebuild acquisition assumptions.** Compare the seller's current bill with an estimated post-transfer bill and stress-test local rates.
3. **Review ownership and renovation plans.** Have counsel and the property appraiser examine potential reassessment triggers before restructuring or committing to major work.
4. **Compare holding and selling on net proceeds.** Include operating costs, capital needs, transaction expenses and your tax adviser's analysis. The assessment cap alone is not a reason to sell, refinance or undertake a 1031 exchange.
