Selling a house with tenants in Florida in 2026 is governed by the state's Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, and three of its rules shape the sale: how security deposits and advance rent pass to the buyer at closing, how much notice ends a tenancy without a fixed term, and when a tenant must allow showings. An owner who settles those three before listing sells with fewer surprises, whether to an investor or to a buyer who wants the home vacant.

A tenant-occupied home is two things at once: a residence someone else is living in, and an asset the owner is trying to sell. *The sale goes smoothly when the plan respects both.*

## Selling a House With Tenants in Florida: Occupied or Vacant

The first decision is which buyer the home is for, because it sets everything else.

| Approach | Who usually buys | What the owner must manage | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sell with the tenant in place | Investors who want the rental income | Lease documents, rent records, the deposit transfer at closing | The buyer pool is limited to purchasers who accept a tenant |
| Deliver the home vacant | Owner-occupants as well as investors | Ending the tenancy lawfully before closing, then preparing the home | Lost rent while vacant, and the timing of the tenancy's end |

Neither is automatically better. A long, well-documented lease at market rent can be an asset to an investor buyer. A month-to-month tenancy in a home that owner-occupants would compete for may support delivering it vacant. The answer depends on the lease, the property and the price each buyer pool will pay.

## What Happens to the Lease When the Property Sells

A sale of the property does not, by itself, end an existing residential lease; the buyer generally takes the property subject to it and steps into the landlord's position. What happens next depends on the lease's own terms, so read it before listing, and look in particular for:

- **The end date** and whether the lease renews.
- **Any notice clause.** Under [section 83.575](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.575), a lease with a specific duration may require notice before it ends, but it may not require less than 30 days' notice or more than 60 days' notice from either party.
- **Any clause about a sale of the property.** Whether a lease allows early termination on sale is a matter of its wording, and a Florida real estate attorney should read it before the owner relies on it.

Investor buyers will ask for the lease, the rent history and confirmation of the deposit held. Asking each tenant to sign an estoppel letter confirming the rent, the term and the deposit puts the buyer, the tenant and the seller on the same figures.

## Ending a Tenancy Without a Fixed Term

When a tenant is on a month-to-month or other periodic arrangement, [section 83.57](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.57) sets the minimum written notice either party must give to end it:

| Tenancy without a specific term | Minimum written notice under s. 83.57 |
|---|---|
| Year to year | 60 days' notice before the end of an annual period |
| Quarter to quarter | 30 days' notice before the end of a quarterly period |
| Month to month | 30 days' notice before the end of a monthly period |
| Week to week | 7 days' notice before the end of a weekly period |

The notice runs to the end of a rental period, not from the day it is given, so the actual move-out date depends on when in the period notice is served. An owner planning to deliver the home vacant should build that calendar into the listing plan before choosing a list date, and should have the form and delivery of the notice checked, because the statute ties it to the method set out in section 83.56(4).

What an owner may not do is equally clear. [Section 83.67](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.67) prohibits a landlord from interrupting utility service or changing the locks to remove a tenant. A sale timeline is never a reason to depart from the lawful process.

## Showing an Occupied Home: What the Access Rules Allow

Buyers need to see the property, and the statute anticipates it. Under [section 83.53](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.53), a tenant may not unreasonably withhold consent for the landlord to enter to exhibit the home to prospective or actual purchasers. For that purpose, the landlord may enter with the tenant's consent, in an emergency, when the tenant unreasonably withholds consent, or when the tenant has been absent for half of a rental period. The statute separately defines reasonable notice and reasonable hours for entry to make repairs. Throughout, the landlord may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant.

In practice, cooperation does more than the statute. Owners who agree a showing schedule with the tenant in advance, give notice of each visit, and keep showings to set windows tend to get a home that is presented well. A tenant who feels ambushed will not help a sale.

## Security Deposits and Advance Rent at Closing

The deposit is the item that needs the most care in a tenant-occupied sale. [Section 83.49(7)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.49) requires that, on the sale or transfer of the rental property, all security deposits and advance rents held for the tenants be transferred to the new owner, together with any earned interest and an accurate accounting of the amount credited to each tenant.

Two further points matter to the seller:

- **The written receipt releases the seller.** Once the funds and records are transferred and the new owner gives a written receipt, the seller is free of the statutory obligation to hold the money for the tenant.
- **The buyer's protection is limited.** The statute creates a rebuttable presumption that the new owner received the deposit from the previous owner, but limits it to 1 month's rent.

The cleanest closing shows the deposit and any advance rent as credits to the buyer on the settlement statement, supported by the accounting and a signed receipt, so that no one is left guessing later who holds the tenant's money.

## Local Rules and State Preemption

South Florida owners often ask whether county or city tenant rules add requirements. [Section 83.425](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/83.425) preempts the regulation of residential tenancies and the landlord-tenant relationship to the state and supersedes local government regulations on the matters the Act covers, including security deposits, the terms of rental agreements and the rights and responsibilities of landlords and tenants. How that applies to a particular local rule is a legal question for an attorney, but the starting point for any tenant-occupied sale is the state statute.

## A Pre-Listing Checklist for a Tenant-Occupied Home

Before the listing goes live, an owner should have each of these in hand:

1. **The signed lease and any renewals or amendments**, read for the end date, notice clause and any sale-related terms.
2. **A rent ledger** showing what has been paid and what, if anything, is owed.
3. **The deposit and advance rent records**, with the accounting the statute requires at transfer.
4. **A decision on occupied or vacant**, and, if vacant, a dated notice plan that follows section 83.57 or the lease.
5. **An agreed showing schedule** with the tenant, consistent with section 83.53.
6. **An estoppel letter** from each tenant, ready for an investor buyer's review.
7. **Tax advice** on the sale of a rental property, including whether a [like-kind exchange](/journal/1031-exchange-south-florida-investment-property-2026) fits the owner's plans.
