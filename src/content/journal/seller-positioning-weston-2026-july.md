Positioning a Weston single-family home before pricing it: the community, not the city, and what buyers verify first.

## What Weston Buyers Are Actually Comparing

Weston reads as one market from the outside and behaves as a dozen from the inside. The community line matters more than the city line.

Buyers here compare a handful of near-identical floor plans across several communities at once, so condition, updates, and lot position carry more weight than square footage alone. The likely buyer here is drawn from relocation, school-driven, and executive-family buyers, and the property is being read against master-planned communities where most homes sit under an association and were built in a compressed window. A list price set without knowing which comparison the property will lose is a guess wearing a number.

## The Diligence That Decides a Weston Deal

The items that most often stall a sale here:

- The community association budget, rules, and any assessment history
- Roof and impact-window condition on original construction
- Permit history for pools, patio enclosures, and window upgrades

None of them improve by waiting, and each is cheaper to resolve on the seller's own schedule than under a contract deadline with a buyer holding the calendar. The specific failure to plan around is an association document set that arrives late and contradicts what the listing implied.

## Where the Price Actually Comes From

Pricing should be built from the specific community rather than the city, lot position and whether it backs to water or preserve, and how completely the interior has actually been updated — not from a per-square-foot figure applied across the city. Overpricing does not create negotiating room. It removes the property from the searches of the buyers most likely to pay full value, and the correction costs more than the original discipline would have.
