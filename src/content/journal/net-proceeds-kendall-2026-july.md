What a Kendall seller keeps depends on the neighborhood, not the wider area — not on the list price alone.

## A List Price Is Not a Net Result

The number on the listing is the first line of an arithmetic problem, not the answer to it. A Kendall owner should review the likely sale range, preparation costs, closing costs, possible credits, association items where they apply, mortgage payoff, timing risk, and the tax questions that belong with a qualified adviser.

## Where the Net Leaks in Kendall

Most of the erosion between list price and net proceeds happens in negotiation, and here it usually traces back to:

- Permit history for additions, enclosed spaces, and in-law conversions
- Association rules where the home sits inside one
- Roof, HVAC, and plumbing age

An item the buyer discovers is worth more to them than the same item disclosed up front, and that difference comes straight out of the seller's net. The recurring cost in this market is unpermitted enclosed space counted as living area in the listing and removed by the appraiser.

## What Holds Value Here

Kendall is not one market. The difference between neighborhoods a few streets apart is real, and buyers know it.

Value follows the specific neighborhood rather than the wider area, lot size and genuinely usable outdoor space, and whether additions were permitted and count as living area. Multigenerational buyers place real value on space that other buyers discount. How the floor plan is described changes which of them shows up. Understanding that lets a seller spend preparation money where it returns something and skip the work that does not move the comparison.
