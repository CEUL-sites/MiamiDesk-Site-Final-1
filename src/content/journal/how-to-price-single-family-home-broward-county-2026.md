How to price a single-family home in Broward County in 2026 starts with three October figures. The median sale price was $610,000, essentially flat against $611,250 a year earlier. Supply stood at 4.4 months, which the association classifies as a seller's market. And the median seller received 96% of the original list price. Scarcity gives the owner leverage; the flat median sets its limit. The opening price has to sit on recent comparable sales, not on the inventory headline.

Fewer homes for sale is a seller's advantage. *It is not a license to price above the evidence.*

## How to Price a Single-Family Home in Broward County: What October 2026 Shows

The [Broward October 2026 report from MIAMI REALTORS® + RWorld](https://www.miamirealtors.com/2026/11/16/broward-real-estate-market-shows-resilience-through-october/), published on November 16, 2026, gives a single-family owner most of what a pricing conversation needs. The figures below cover Broward County single-family homes only:

| Broward single-family, October 2026 | This year | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median sale price | $610,000 | $611,250 | down 0.20% |
| Closed sales | 892 | 1,037 | down 13.9% |
| Dollar volume | $770 million | | down 7.29% |
| Active listings | 4,467 | 5,364 | down 16.72% |
| Months of supply | 4.4 | | seller's market |
| Median days, listing to contract | 41 | 55 | faster |
| Median days, listing to closing | 78 | 92 | faster |
| Median percent of original list price received | 96% | | |
| Cash share of sales | 25.4% | | |

Year to date, single-family sales are still ahead: 10,264 closings from January through October, up 2.8% from 9,978 a year earlier. October was a slower month inside a year that has held up.

Two things are happening at once. Inventory is shrinking faster than sales, which is why supply tightened to 4.4 months. The report describes a balanced market as six to nine months of supply, so Broward single-family sits well on the seller's side of that line. At the same time, the median price did not move. Buyers are still buying, and they are buying faster, but they are not paying more.

## Why Shrinking Inventory Has Not Lifted the Broward Median

The usual expectation is that fewer listings push prices up. In Broward this autumn, financing costs have absorbed that pressure. The 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.11% in the [Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey of November 12, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/12/3402013/0/en/mortgage-rates-decline-for-fourth-consecutive-week-to-7-11.html), down from 7.18% the week before but well above the 6.24% of a year earlier. With only 25.4% of Broward single-family sales paid in cash in October, most buyers finance, and for them the monthly payment, not the seller's asking price, sets the ceiling.

The repeat-sales data tells the same story more precisely. The median price can move simply because a different mix of homes sold that month. The Federal Housing Finance Agency's all-transactions index tracks the same properties as they resell, so it isolates price change:

| FHFA all-transactions index | Reading | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| [Fort Lauderdale-Pompano Beach-Sunrise metropolitan division](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ATNHPIUS22744Q), Q2 2025 | 544.65 | Starting point |
| Same series, Q4 2025 | 550.98 | Highest of the last five quarters |
| Same series, Q2 2026 | 548.87 | Up 0.77% in a year, below Q4 2025 |
| [Broward County annual index](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ATNHPIUS12011A), 2024 to 2025 | 415.24 to 417.12 | Up 0.45% |
| Broward County annual index, 2021 to 2025 | 289.27 to 417.12 | Up 44.20% |

Read together, the figures say that Broward single-family values rose sharply earlier in the decade and have been close to level since. The metropolitan division index rose 49.82% between Q2 2021 (366.35) and Q2 2026, but nearly all of that came before 2025. The index covers houses and condos together, and FHFA describes the annual county series as developmental, so treat it as context rather than as a valuation of any one home.

For an owner, the practical meaning is simple. A list price built on the appreciation of 2021 and 2022 is pricing a market that has already paused. *Equity was built in the earlier run-up. The sale price is set by this year's buyers.*

## What 96% of Original List Price Means for Your Opening Number

The median percent of original list price received is the most useful single number for a seller setting a first price. It compares what a home finally sold for with the price it first went on the market at, so it captures every reduction along the way.

At 96% in October, half of Broward single-family sales closed at 96% of their first list price or less. That is a narrow gap, and it reflects a market where well-priced homes are going under contract in a median of 41 days. It also shows where the negotiation happens: in the first price, not in the final offer.

Two patterns follow from that:

- **Homes priced at the evidence** tend to sell near their list price, because buyers and their agents recognize the comparison and act before a competing buyer does.
- **Homes priced above the evidence** tend to sit, reduce, and then sell below what a well-set first price would have drawn, because by the time the price is corrected the listing is no longer new to the agents who work the market every day.

The contrast with condos makes the point. Broward condos had 10.6 months of supply in October and sellers received a median 92% of original list price, which we compared in detail in [Selling a House vs. a Condo in South Florida](/journal/selling-house-vs-condo-south-florida-2026). Single-family owners hold far more leverage. They keep it by not spending it on an opening price buyers will not pay.

## How to Set the List Price: A Five-Step Method

Our team uses the same sequence for every single-family pricing review in Broward. It is built for a flat-price, low-supply market like the one the October report describes.

1. **Start with closed sales, not active listings.** Active listings show what other owners hope to receive. Closed sales in the same subdivision or immediate neighborhood, adjusted for size, lot, age and condition, show what buyers have actually paid.
2. **Weight the most recent closings.** With the county median flat and the repeat-sales index slightly below its late-2025 reading, a sale from early in 2025 can overstate today's value. The newer the comparable, the more weight it carries.
3. **Price the full monthly cost, not just the house.** Buyers financing at current rates calculate payment, insurance and taxes before they offer. A current four-point inspection and wind-mitigation report let them do that early, as we explained in [Insurability Before Price](/journal/insurability-roof-wind-mitigation-south-florida-sale-2026).
4. **Read the competition in your price band.** County supply of 4.4 months is an average. Some price bands and neighborhoods are tighter and some are looser. Count the active listings a buyer would see alongside yours, and note how long they have been on the market.
5. **Agree on the review point before launch.** Decide in advance what level of showings and agent feedback would confirm the price, and what would call for an adjustment. A decision made before listing is a strategy; a decision made after weeks without offers is a reaction.

## Weston, Plantation and Fort Lauderdale: The County Median Is Only a Starting Point

A $610,000 county median blends very different markets. A four-bedroom pool home in a Weston gated community, a renovated ranch home in Plantation and a waterfront property in Fort Lauderdale each draw a different buyer, compare against different sales, and carry different insurance and flood considerations.

That is why we never price from the county number. It tells an owner the direction of the market: tight supply, flat prices, faster contracts. The street, the subdivision and the condition of the home tell an owner the price. ## How to Tell Whether the Price Is Working

Once a home is listed, the market gives its answer through agents.

- **Showings with offers, or showings with agent feedback that names a number,** mean the price is being taken seriously.
- **Showings without offers** usually mean buyers like the home and disagree with the price.
- **Few showings** usually mean the price is filtering the home out of buyers' searches before anyone sees it.

The October median of 41 days from listing to contract is a reference point, not a deadline. A home that has drawn neither offers nor serious interest well past that point is telling its owner something about price, condition or presentation. The earlier that message is heard, the less it costs.
