## Who the long-distance buyer is

A meaningful share of South Florida buyers do not live here when they start their search. Some are relocating for work or family from another state. Some are buying a second home. Some live abroad and are buying a residence, a base for children at university, or an investment. What they have in common is distance: they shop from far away, visit on short trips, and depend heavily on their agent.

For a seller, the useful question is not where these buyers come from. It is what a buyer who cannot drop by on a Saturday needs before making an offer, and how the listing reaches the agents who represent them.

## What long-distance buyers prioritize

- **Turnkey presentation.** Buyers purchasing from a distance, often during a short visit, tend to prefer homes that need no immediate work. A well-prepared home is easier to buy remotely than an equivalent home that needs renovation.
- **Complete documentation, up front.** HOA or condo financials, reserve information, permits, and disclosures answer questions that a distant buyer cannot resolve by visiting again. Missing documents become negotiating leverage at the end of the deal.
- **Building and community quality signals.** Recognized management, visible maintenance, amenities, schools, parks and commute access all carry weight when a buyer is comparing several cities on one trip.
- **A process that works remotely.** Video walkthroughs, digital signatures, and a title company experienced with remote closings make a listing easier to act on.
- **Financing clarity.** Not every buyer pays cash. Some use mortgage programs for buyers without U.S. credit history, which can carry different timelines and documentation. A listing agent who reads those offers correctly protects the seller.
- **Service in the buyer's language.** A transaction handled clearly in English, and in Spanish when that helps, reduces friction for every party.

## How a South Florida seller reaches these buyers

Long-distance buyers almost always work through an agent, so the listing has to reach those agents where they work:

- **The MLS.** An eligible listing enters the Miami and South Florida REALTORS® MLS, the daily working ecosystem of its 93,000 association members.
- **Syndication.** Eligible listings can be distributed across 200+ global portals in 19 languages, where many long-distance buyers begin their research.
- **The international referral network.** The association's 437+ international agreements, across partner markets in 75+ countries, connect South Florida listings with agents who represent buyers abroad. For owners who live outside the United States, the [Global Desk](/global-desk) coordinates the listing in two languages and two time zones.

## A note for owners who live abroad

South Florida also has many owners who live outside the United States and are now evaluating a sale. FIRPTA (the Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act) generally requires withholding on sales by non-U.S. persons, with the rate depending on the price and on how the buyer will use the property. Our [FIRPTA note for foreign sellers](/journal/firpta-withholding-foreign-seller-south-florida-2026) explains the basics. Consult a qualified U.S. tax attorney or CPA before listing; that is outside what a real estate agent can advise on.
