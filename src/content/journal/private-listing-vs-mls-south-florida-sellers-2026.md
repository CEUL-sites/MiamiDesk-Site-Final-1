A private listing vs MLS decision in South Florida in 2026 is a decision about who is allowed to evaluate the property. On the Miami MLS, a standard listing is distributed to the agents who work in that system and, in most cases, onward to public websites. An office exclusive is filed with the MLS but withheld from other agents and from the public, and the seller signs a form acknowledging the exposure being waived. Privacy is the gain. A smaller pool of agents and buyers is the cost.

Privacy is a legitimate objective. *It is also a choice about how many qualified buyers ever learn the property exists.*

## What Is a Private Listing vs MLS Listing in South Florida?

"Private listing," "pocket listing," "off-market" and "private exclusive" are marketing terms. The rules that govern them use narrower language, and the difference matters when a seller is asked to sign.

The national baseline is the [Clear Cooperation Policy of the National Association of REALTORS®](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-clear-cooperation-policy). Under it, a listing broker who markets a property to the public must submit the listing to the MLS within one business day, for cooperation with other MLS participants. The policy defines public marketing broadly: flyers displayed in windows, yard signs, digital marketing on public-facing websites, brokerage website displays, email blasts, multi-brokerage listing sharing networks and applications available to the general public.

In March 2025, the association kept that policy in place and added a second one, [Multiple Listing Options for Sellers](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/multiple-listing-options-for-sellers), which MLSs were required to implement by the end of September 2025. It recognizes two kinds of exempt listing:

- **Office exclusive.** The seller directs that the property not be disseminated through the MLS and not be publicly marketed. The listing is still filed with the MLS, but it is not distributed to other participants.
- **Delayed marketing.** The seller directs the listing broker to delay public marketing through IDX, the feed that displays listings on brokerage websites, and through syndication, for a period the local MLS allows. During the delay, the listing remains available to other MLS participants through the MLS platform.

Seller disclosure is required for both. The policy calls for a certification in which the seller acknowledges the MLS benefits being waived or delayed. The same policy notes that one-to-one, broker-to-broker conversations about a listing do not trigger the submission requirement, while multi-brokerage communications count as public marketing.

So a private listing is not a listing that sits outside the rules. *It is a listing that sits inside them, under a status the seller has chosen in writing.*

## The Four Ways a Listing Can Sit in the Miami MLS

National policy leaves the detail to each MLS. For South Florida, that detail is in the [MLS Rules and Regulations](https://www.miamirealtors.com/mls/mls-rules-and-regulations/) of the Miami MLS, which the rulebook calls the Southeast Florida Multiple Listing Service (SEFMLS). The version read for this article is marked as last updated in August 2025. Listing agreements in the market area are to be filed with and entered into the MLS within two business days of the agreement's effective date (section 15.1.1), and the rules then give an owner four practical positions:

1. **Active, with internet display.** The listing is distributed to MLS participants and subscribers and can be displayed publicly online. This is the default, and the position against which the others are measured.
2. **Active, with internet display withheld.** The association's seller form describes an option labeled "Active – Internet N." Information about the property is not publicly displayed or distributed on the internet or on third-party websites, but it is still shared with other MLS participants and subscribers, who are licensed real estate professionals and can share it with their own customers. Separately, an owner may ask in the listing agreement that the property address be withheld from online publication to third parties.
3. **Coming Soon.** A property can hold this status for up to 21 days (section 4.5). Public marketing is permitted, but showings and open houses are not, and days on market do not accrue. Offers may be presented and accepted. A signed seller authorization form, numbered MIAMI-11, is required.
4. **Office Exclusive.** The owner has been advised in writing of the benefits of the MLS and instructs the listing broker, also in writing, that the listing should not be published and distributed publicly or to other participants (section 6.6). Submission to the MLS is still mandatory. Showings are permitted, public marketing for open houses is not, and days on market do not accrue. A signed seller authorization form, numbered MIAMI-10, is required. If someone asks about a specific address, the MLS will disclose whether a listing for that address has been filed as an office exclusive.

The second position deserves more attention than it usually receives. It removes the property from public websites while keeping it in front of the professionals who represent buyers.

## What the Seller Signs, and What the Form Says About Price

The office exclusive form is short, and it is unusually direct. The [Seller Authorization Form for Office Exclusive Listings](https://www.miamirealtors.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2025/09/Seller-Authorization-Form-Office-Exclusives.pdf) is signed by the seller and the listing broker and sent to the MLS. In it, the seller acknowledges several things:

- That the seller wants marketing restricted or delayed, and that this limits broad access to information and, in the form's words, "may affect the sale price."
- That other real estate licensees representing prospective buyers may not be aware the property is available.
- That withholding the listing from other brokers and the public, again in the form's words, "may substantially reduce the number of local, national, and foreign potential buyers/renters."
- That the seller is knowingly waiving the exposure the MLS provides, and by extension the exposure of third-party websites and services.
- That the "Active – Internet N" option exists, and that an office exclusive would not provide the professional sharing that option keeps.
- That the seller may, at any time, instruct the listing broker to enter the listing into the MLS.

The form also records that the association does not charge the seller for the listing to be entered. The broker, for its part, acknowledges that an office exclusive does not relieve it of its obligations under the MLS rules.

An owner who is offered a private listing should read this form before the listing agreement, not after. *The document that authorizes the restriction is also the clearest statement of what the restriction costs.*

## Does a Private Listing Sell for More or for Less?

The published research does not agree, and a seller should know that before accepting a confident answer in either direction.

A national brokerage's own study found that homes it pre-marketed privately sold for more. A listing portal's study, [released in May 2026](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-14-Selling-with-one-agent-on-both-sides-cost-home-sellers-1-49-billion-over-three-years), found that sellers who never listed on the MLS typically sold for less than sellers who listed publicly. An MLS-sponsored analysis pointed the same way as the portal's. An academic study of Dallas–Fort Worth sales by a University of Georgia professor, which was not commissioned by a third party, found a premium for off-MLS sales in older data that was no longer statistically significant after the Clear Cooperation Policy took effect, as [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/04/01/pocket-listings-really-do-sell-for-more-at-least-in-dallas) and [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/04/08/homes-marketed-off-mls-get-better-prices-independent-study-finds/) reported in April 2026. A [HousingWire column](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/off-mls-antitrust-scrutiny/) from August 2026 sets the sponsored studies side by side.

Two cautions follow. Most of these studies were produced by organizations with a commercial position in the debate. And none of them measures a specific property in Weston, Coral Gables or Brickell. A study average is not a pricing analysis.

## When Does a Private Listing Fit?

There are sound reasons to limit exposure:

- **Personal security or public profile.** An owner whose name, address or interior photographs should not circulate has a real interest that outweighs maximum exposure.
- **Constrained access.** A tenant-occupied property, an estate still being settled or a household in transition may not be able to accommodate open-market showing traffic.
- **A property that is not ready.** Repairs, staging and photography take time. Coming Soon status exists for this purpose and carries its own limit.
- **A confidential sale.** A separation, a business event or a relocation the owner has not announced can justify discretion for a defined period.

A private listing fits poorly when the owner's main objective is price. Price is discovered by competition among informed buyers, and an office exclusive, by design, reduces the number of agents who know the property is for sale. It also fits poorly when the restriction serves someone other than the owner. The useful question is plain: whose objective does the limit on exposure advance?

## Why Is This Question in the News?

The rules themselves are being contested. In the second week of November 2026, a large national brokerage confirmed a written broker-to-broker cooperation policy for its agents, covering its private and coming-soon listings, under which agents cooperate with other brokers unless the client instructs otherwise in writing, according to [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/11/compass-makes-broker-to-broker-cooperation-rules-official) and [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/11/11/compass-broker-cooperation-private-listings-bright-mls/). The same brokerage and a number of MLSs are in litigation over rules that restrict marketing outside the MLS, as [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-broker-cooperation-policy/) and [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/11/12/compass-sues-15-mlses-over-listing-policies/) reported that week.

For a South Florida owner, the national dispute changes little today. The rule that governs a listing here is the local MLS rule, and the form the owner signs. Owners who are pitched a private listing while the debate is in the headlines should ask to see both.

## Questions to Ask Before You Agree to a Private Listing

1. Under which MLS status will my listing be filed, and may I read the seller form before I sign the listing agreement?
2. Who will be told about the property, and how: agents at other brokerages, or only agents inside this firm?
3. Would withholding internet display, or withholding the address, give me the privacy I need while keeping buyer agents informed?
4. If the buyer is also represented inside the listing firm, how is the brokerage relationship with each side disclosed and documented?
5. What is the plan, and the trigger, for moving the property into full MLS distribution if the private period does not produce an acceptable offer?
6. Without open-market exposure, what evidence will support the price: which comparable sales, and which competing active listings?

An agent with a considered private strategy can answer all six in writing. For the wider selection process, see our guide to [choosing a listing agent in South Florida](/journal/how-to-choose-a-listing-agent-south-florida-2026).

## Where Distribution Fits

Features describe a property. Distribution determines who evaluates it. Listing on the Miami MLS places the property inside the daily working ecosystem of 93,000 Miami and South Florida REALTORS® members (membership reported in May 2026), who can find it and present it to their own buyers. That is the benefit an office exclusive sets aside, and the reason the form asks the seller to acknowledge it. We explained the mechanics in [Listing a Home Online vs. Activating the Market](/journal/listing-online-vs-activating-the-market-south-florida-2026) and in [Your Highest-Price Buyer Already Has an Agent](/journal/highest-price-buyer-agent-network-south-florida-2026). Distribution does not guarantee a price or a timeline. It determines how many qualified agents and buyers are in a position to compete for the property.
