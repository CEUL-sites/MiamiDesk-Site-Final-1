A Florida listing agreement is the written contract that authorizes a brokerage to market and sell your property. In 2026, Florida law requires it to state a definite expiration date, the property, the price and terms, the fee, and your signature, and it forbids a clause requiring you to give notice to end it after that date. What the law leaves open, from term length to marketing commitments, is what an owner should negotiate before signing.

Most owners read the price and the fee and sign the rest. *The rest decides how the sale is run.*

## What Florida Law Requires in a Listing Agreement

The requirements sit in the licensing chapter rather than in contract law. [Section 475.25(1)(r)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/475.25) makes it a ground for discipline when a licensee's written listing agreement fails to include the items below, or fails to give the owner a copy.

| Requirement under s. 475.25(1)(r) | What it means for the owner |
|---|---|
| A definite expiration date | The agreement must end on a stated date; an open-ended listing does not meet the statute |
| A description of the property | The agreement identifies exactly what is being listed |
| Price and terms | The asking price and the terms on which you will sell are written down |
| Fee or commission | The compensation is stated in the agreement itself |
| A proper signature of the principal | Every owner who must sign has signed |
| A legible, signed, true and correct copy to the owner | You receive your own signed copy promptly, within the time the statute sets |
| No notice required to cancel after expiration | The agreement may not require you to notify the broker of an intention to cancel after the expiration date |

The last line matters most in practice. A listing that renews itself unless the owner objects in writing is the clause the statute rules out: once the expiration date passes, the owner does not have to give notice to end it. Continuing the listing after that date takes a new, signed extension.

## Who You Are Contracting With: The Brokerage, Not Only the Agent

An owner usually chooses a person. The contract, however, runs to that person's brokerage.

Under [section 475.42(1)(d)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/475.42), a sales associate may not collect any money in a real estate brokerage transaction, whether as a commission, deposit or otherwise, except in the name of the employer and with the employer's express consent. A sales associate also cannot sue for a commission against anyone other than the brokerage that employed them when the work was done.

Two consequences follow. The listing agreement names the brokerage, and the brokerage's policies, supervision and escrow practices stand behind the sale. And when you evaluate an agent, you are also evaluating the firm: its size, its support for marketing and transaction management, and the reach it brings to your listing.

## Transaction Broker or Single Agent: How You Will Be Represented

Florida allows two forms of representation, and the default applies unless you agree otherwise in writing.

[Section 475.278](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/475.278) presumes that every licensee is operating as a transaction broker unless a single agent relationship, or no brokerage relationship, is established in writing. The same section prohibits disclosed and nondisclosed dual agency: no licensee may represent both the buyer and the seller as a fiduciary.

| | Transaction broker | Single agent |
|---|---|---|
| Representation | Limited representation of the buyer, the seller or both; not a fiduciary | Represents only you, as a fiduciary |
| Core duties | Dealing honestly and fairly, accounting for all funds, skill, care and diligence, presenting all offers and counteroffers, disclosing known facts that materially affect the value of residential property and are not readily observable | All of those, plus loyalty, confidentiality, obedience and full disclosure |
| Confidentiality | Limited: the licensee may not reveal that you would accept less than the listed price, or your motivation for selling, unless you waive it in writing | Full confidentiality |

A single agent relationship can be changed to a transaction broker relationship during the engagement, but only with the owner's prior written consent, and the statute requires the single agent disclosure to be made before or at the time the listing agreement is signed, or before a showing, whichever comes first. Read which form your agreement establishes and what the disclosure in it says. Both are legitimate; they differ in how far the licensee's loyalty runs.

## Exclusive Right of Sale or Exclusive Agency

Listing agreements also differ in when the fee is earned.

- **Exclusive right of sale.** The listing brokerage is entitled to its fee if the property sells during the term, regardless of who finds the buyer. It gives the brokerage the certainty to invest in marketing the property.
- **Exclusive agency.** The brokerage has the exclusive right to market the property, but if the owner finds the buyer personally, no listing fee is owed.

The choice changes the incentives. An owner who already has a likely buyer in mind should say so before signing and write that buyer into the agreement as an exclusion, rather than rely on an exclusive agency form alone. ## Florida Listing Agreement Terms to Negotiate Before You Sign

The statute sets the minimum. The commercial terms are for the owner and the brokerage to agree. Before signing, an owner should be able to answer each of these from the document itself:

1. **How long is the term, and does it fit the plan?** A term long enough for a full marketing cycle protects the work; a term with no review point leaves an underperforming listing in place.
2. **What happens if the price needs to change?** Agree in advance when the pricing will be reviewed against showings, feedback and new comparable sales.
3. **What is the fee, and how is the buyer's side handled?** The agreement should state your fee and how any compensation offered to a buyer's brokerage will be decided and documented.
4. **What marketing is committed in writing?** Photography, MLS entry, the portals and the agent outreach you are paying for should be described, not assumed.
5. **Is there a protection period after expiration?** Some agreements keep the fee payable if the property sells after expiration to a buyer introduced during the term. Know how long that period runs and how the buyers are identified.
6. **How can the agreement end early?** The statute requires a definite end date; it does not create a right to end the agreement before that date. Read the termination clause and know what a withdrawal costs before you need it.
7. **Which representation form applies?** Confirm transaction broker or single agent, and keep the signed disclosure.

## What a Listing Agreement Does Not Change

Signing a listing agreement does not shift the owner's own obligations. Disclosure is the clearest example: under section 475.278, both forms of representation require the licensee to disclose known facts that materially affect the value of residential property and are not readily observable, and owners carry their own disclosure obligations under Florida law, including the flood disclosure covered in our [guide for home sellers](/journal/florida-flood-disclosure-home-sellers-2026). A good listing agreement assigns the work; it does not remove the duty. Questions about what a particular fact requires belong with a Florida real estate attorney.

## Where Distribution Fits

The listing agreement is where an owner decides how the property will reach the market. Listing on the Miami MLS places the property inside the daily working ecosystem of 93,000 Miami and South Florida REALTORS® members, who can find it and present it to their own buyers. According to Miami and South Florida REALTORS®, association listings are featured on 200+ global portals in 19 languages, although not every property appears on every website or app and the sites may change, and they are shared with 260+ U.S. MLSs through RPR; the association also reports 437+ international agreements with partner associations and 11 MLS data exchanges. That reach is what an exclusive right of sale agreement should buy, and the agreement should describe it. Distribution does not guarantee a price or a timeline. It determines how many qualified agents and buyers are in a position to compete for the property.
