Staying in a Kendall property after closing: negotiate it alongside the neighborhood, not the wider area, before the offer.

## Decide Early Whether You Need Time

Some sellers need to stay in the property after closing. That is often workable, but it should not be improvised once a buyer has already submitted an offer. Post-occupancy touches buyer confidence, lender timing, insurance, escrow, walkthrough expectations, and leverage all at once.

## What Else Is Already on the Table in Kendall

A buyer weighing a possession request weighs it alongside everything else the property is asking them to accept. Here that usually includes:

- Permit history for additions, enclosed spaces, and in-law conversions
- Association rules where the home sits inside one
- Roof, HVAC, and plumbing age

A clean file on those items buys credibility for the possession conversation; an unresolved one spends it — particularly where the open question is unpermitted enclosed space counted as living area in the listing and removed by the appraiser.

## Prepare the Terms Before the Offer

Kendall is not one market. The difference between neighborhoods a few streets apart is real, and buyers know it.

Before listing, decide whether flexibility is genuinely required, how much time is needed, what terms are acceptable, and which points need legal review. Multigenerational buyers place real value on space that other buyers discount. How the floor plan is described changes which of them shows up. The listing strategy should anticipate the buyer's risk questions instead of meeting them as objections.

## FAQ

### Is post-occupancy common in Kendall transactions?

It appears regularly, particularly where a seller is coordinating a second purchase. It is negotiable rather than standard, and the terms — duration, escrow, insurance, condition at handover — should be documented and reviewed with a licensed professional.
