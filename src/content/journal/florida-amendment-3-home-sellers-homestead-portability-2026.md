Florida Amendment 3 raises the homestead exemption, and for home sellers the question is what happens to that benefit when they sell and buy again. Voters approved it on November 3, 2026, and it takes effect on January 1, 2027. County property appraisers state that it does not change Save Our Homes or portability. For South Florida home sellers in 2026, the text reserves the smaller, newcomer exemption for people who had no permanent Florida residence as of December 31, 2026.

The headline is a larger exemption. *The seller's question is what travels with the owner and what stays with the house.*

## What Florida Amendment 3 Means for Home Sellers in 2026

Amendment 3 is the ballot number for [CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF), a joint resolution that amends the property tax article of the Florida Constitution. The [Volusia County Property Appraiser](https://vcpa.vcgov.org/info/amendment3) and the [Leon County Property Appraiser](https://www.leonpa.gov/Amendment3) both state that voters approved it on November 3, 2026 and that it takes effect on January 1, 2027. Leon County's office adds that it does not affect 2026 property tax bills. The changes first appear on the August 2027 notices of proposed property taxes and on the bills mailed in November 2027.

For an owner who lives in the home, the enrolled text does three things:

- **It raises the homestead exemption on non-school levies.** For all levies other than school district levies, the exemption is up to $150,000 of assessed value beginning January 1, 2027, and up to $250,000 beginning January 1, 2028. Both county offices describe an annual inflation adjustment from 2029.
- **It leaves the school-levy exemption where it was.** For school district levies, the exemption remains up to $25,000 of assessed value.
- **It creates a separate, smaller tier for newcomers.** A person who had not maintained a permanent residence in Florida as of December 31, 2026 is exempt up to $50,000 for non-school levies, and moves to the larger amount beginning with the fifth year of exemption.

For property that is not a homestead, the same resolution lowers the limit on annual assessment increases from 10% to 5% beginning January 1, 2027, for non-school levies. We covered that side in our guide for [investors and landlords](/journal/florida-amendment-3-property-tax-investors-landlords-2027).

Two cautions belong next to those numbers. An exemption reduces the assessed value that is taxed. It is not a credit against the bill, and it does not set the tax rate. And the Volusia County office states that actual savings cannot be determined until implementing legislation is finalized and future tax rates are established.

## The Date That Sorts Owners Into Two Groups

The date is December 31, 2026. The smaller exemption tier applies to a person "who had not maintained a permanent residence in this state as of December 31, 2026." The general clause, which carries the larger amounts, has no such condition.

Both county offices describe the dividing line the same way. Residential property owners who are permanent Florida residents as of December 31, 2026 are eligible for the larger exemption amounts beginning in 2027, subject to the requirements of the Florida Constitution. Both add a point that matters to anyone between homes: a person who was a permanent Florida resident by that date, but did not own a home, is eligible for the higher amount when they later purchase a residential property and apply for homestead.

For newcomers, both offices state that after maintaining a Florida homestead exemption for four years, the owner becomes eligible for the larger exemption on January 1 of the fifth year. The text also lets a county or municipality, by a two-thirds vote of its governing body, reduce that five-year requirement for a critical local need, beginning in 2030.

As written, the December 31 test is about the person and the state. *It is not written around a particular parcel.*

## If You Sell and Buy Another Florida Home, Which Exemption Applies?

This is the seller's version of the question, and it deserves a precise answer in two parts.

**What the text says.** The general clause covers every person who holds title to real estate and maintains a permanent residence on it. The smaller tier is limited to a person who had not maintained a permanent residence in Florida as of December 31, 2026. A separate sentence allows not more than one exemption to any individual or family unit, so the exemption follows the permanent residence, not the number of properties owned.

**What the text does not say.** No sentence in the resolution addresses an owner who sells one Florida homestead and establishes another after the amendment takes effect. Neither the Volusia County nor the Leon County Amendment 3 page answers that scenario directly.

Read together, the text and the appraisers' guidance point one way: an owner who was a permanent Florida resident on December 31, 2026 is not the person the newcomer tier describes. That is a reading of the text. It is not a ruling. The Volusia County office states that the amendment requires implementing legislation, and the Leon County office says it is awaiting additional guidance from the State of Florida on eligibility verification and exemption administration.

One point is settled either way. The exemption on the next home is not automatic. It needs its own application, and the [Broward County Property Appraiser](https://bcpa.net/) lists March 1, 2027 as the end of the timely filing period for a 2027 homestead exemption.

## What Does Not Change: Save Our Homes and Portability

Both county offices are direct on this. Amendment 3 "does not eliminate or replace Save Our Homes," and it "does not change Florida's portability provisions." The constitutional text printed in the resolution keeps the familiar rules:

- **The annual limit.** Changes in a homestead's assessment may not exceed the lower of 3% of the prior year's assessment or the percent change in the Consumer Price Index.
- **The reset on sale.** After a change of ownership, homestead property is assessed at just value as of January 1 of the following year, unless portability applies.
- **Portability.** An owner who establishes a new homestead, and who received a homestead exemption as of January 1 of any of the three years immediately before it, may have the new homestead assessed at less than just value. When the new home's just value is equal to or greater than the prior home's, the reduction is the lesser of $500,000 or the difference between the prior home's just value and its assessed value. When the new home's just value is lower, the benefit is scaled in proportion, with the same $500,000 limit.

For a long-held home, the gap between just value and assessed value can be large, and portability is the mechanism that carries part of it to the next home. Our guide to [Florida homestead and Save Our Homes portability](/journal/florida-homestead-portability-benefits-2026) sets out the filing steps.

The appraiser pages reviewed for this article do not explain how the larger exemption and a ported assessment combine on a specific bill. That calculation belongs to the county property appraiser's office, parcel by parcel.

## What Travels, What Resets and What Is Still Open

**Travels with the owner**

- Florida permanent residency as of December 31, 2026, the date the text uses to separate the two exemption tiers.
- The Save Our Homes difference, through portability, up to $500,000, when the new homestead is established inside the three-year window.

**Resets with the sale**

- The assessed value of the home being sold. After a change of ownership it is assessed at just value as of January 1 of the following year.
- The homestead exemption itself. The next home needs its own application with the county property appraiser.
- The buyer's tax position, which depends on the buyer's own residency and use of the property.

**Still open**

- Administrative procedures. Both county offices say additional procedures may be established through implementing legislation.
- Documentation. The Leon County office says additional guidance may be necessary to determine whether owners will need to provide documentation or take other steps for the increased exemption.
- Dollar savings. They depend on the property's value, exemption eligibility and the millage rates local taxing authorities adopt.

## What Your Buyer Will Ask About Property Taxes

The seller's tax bill is not the buyer's tax bill. After Amendment 3, three different buyers can stand in front of the same house with three different answers.

1. **The Florida resident.** A buyer who was a permanent Florida resident as of December 31, 2026 and applies for homestead is in the larger exemption tier. The property itself is still assessed at just value after the change of ownership, unless the buyer brings portability from a prior homestead.
2. **The newcomer.** A buyer who establishes Florida residency later starts in the $50,000 tier for non-school levies and reaches the larger amount in the fifth year of exemption.
3. **The second-home or investment buyer.** No homestead exemption applies. For residential property of nine units or fewer, annual assessment increases for non-school levies are limited to 5% beginning January 1, 2027. School levies are outside that limit.

A listing that quotes last year's tax bill without context invites a surprise during the buyer's underwriting. County offices are publishing tools that close the gap. The [Miami-Dade County Property Appraiser](https://www.miamidadepa.gov/pa/home.page) site points owners to 2027 and 2028 Amendment 3 estimates, and the [Palm Beach County Property Appraiser](https://pbcpao.gov/calc-amend3-tax.htm) has posted an Amendment 3 homestead calculator.

A tax figure the buyer verifies before the offer supports the price. *One discovered after the offer becomes a reason to reopen it.*

## Before Year-End: A Seller's Sequence

Amendment 3 alone is not a reason to sell, and it is not a reason to wait. It changes the inputs. An owner weighing a sale and a purchase in Florida can put them in order:

1. **Confirm the current record.** Check that the homestead exemption on the present home is in place and correctly recorded with the county property appraiser.
2. **Read the assessment.** Find the just value and the assessed value on the county record. The difference is the Save Our Homes amount that portability may carry forward.
3. **Map the purchase window.** Set the intended purchase date against the three-year portability window.
4. **Calendar the next application.** The homestead exemption on the new home requires its own filing with the county property appraiser.
5. **Put the question in writing.** Ask the appraiser's office in the county of the next home how it will document residency as of December 31, 2026, and keep the reply with the closing file.
6. **Model both sides together.** Net proceeds from the sale and carrying costs on the next home belong in one calculation. Our guide to [what it costs to sell a home in South Florida](/journal/seller-closing-costs-south-florida-2026) lists the seller-side line items. Tax and legal questions go to a CPA and a Florida real estate attorney.

Nothing in this sequence changes a 2026 tax bill. The first notices that reflect Amendment 3 are the ones issued in August 2027.
