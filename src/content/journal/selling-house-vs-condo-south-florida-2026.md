Selling a house vs. a condo in South Florida in 2026 means selling into two different markets. In October 2026, Miami-Dade single-family homes had 5.4 months of supply and a median price of $706,000, up 3.52% from a year earlier. Miami-Dade condos had 12.7 months of supply, and the Broward condo median fell 7.22%. The same county, the same mortgage rates, and two different negotiating positions for the seller.

South Florida is not one housing market. *The property type decides which market an owner is selling into.*

## Selling a House vs. a Condo in South Florida: What the October Data Shows

The association's October 2026 reports for [Miami-Dade](https://www.miamirealtors.com/2026/11/16/miami-dade-home-sales-continue-to-outpace-last-year/) and [Broward](https://www.miamirealtors.com/2026/11/16/broward-real-estate-market-shows-resilience-through-october/), released on November 16, 2026, put the divergence in plain numbers:

| Segment, October 2026 | Median price | Change from a year earlier | Months of supply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade single-family | $706,000 | up 3.52% from $682,000 | 5.4 (seller's market) |
| Miami-Dade condos | $397,500 | down 0.63% from $400,000 | 12.7 (buyer's market) |
| Broward single-family | $610,000 | down 0.20% from $611,250 | 4.4 (seller's market) |
| Broward condos | $240,300 | down 7.22% from $259,000 | 10.6 (buyer's market) |

Activity cooled in both counties. Miami-Dade closed 1,689 sales in October, down 10.6% from 1,890 a year earlier, though January-through-October sales of 18,494 were still up 4%. Broward closed 1,750 sales, down 12.9%. Mortgage costs are part of the reason: the 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.11% in the [Freddie Mac survey of November 12, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/12/3402013/0/en/mortgage-rates-decline-for-fourth-consecutive-week-to-7-11.html), down for a fourth consecutive week but well above the 6.24% of a year earlier.

Months of supply is the number to watch. It measures how long current listings would take to sell at the current pace. The association's own reports describe the single-family figures of 5.4 and 4.4 months as seller's markets, and the condo figures of 12.7 and 10.6 months as buyer's markets.

## Selling a Single-Family Home: Why Supply Keeps Leverage Intact

Single-family owners in Weston, Coral Gables, Fort Lauderdale and comparable neighborhoods sell into short supply. In Miami-Dade, single-family active listings fell 13.81% year over year; in Broward, they fell 16.72%. Broward single-family sellers received a median 96% of original list price in October.

Tight supply does not mean any price will hold. The Broward single-family median was essentially flat, which says buyers are paying for well-presented homes and resisting the rest. Three preparations matter most:

1. **Insurance readiness.** Buyers calculate the full monthly cost, including windstorm insurance, before they write an offer. A current four-point inspection and a wind-mitigation report let a buyer obtain quotes early, rather than discovering a problem during underwriting. We covered this in detail in [Insurability, Roofs and Wind Mitigation](/journal/insurability-roof-wind-mitigation-south-florida-sale-2026).
2. **Pricing to evidence.** With 30-year rates averaging 7.11%, a list price above recent comparable sales tends to sit. A price anchored to current absorption in the neighborhood draws the buyers who are ready to act.
3. **Carrying-cost transparency.** A buyer's property tax bill is not the seller's bill, particularly where the seller has held a homestead exemption for years. Showing the buyer a realistic estimate early prevents a late renegotiation.

## Selling a Condo: Reserves, Assessments and Financing

Condo owners face a different set of questions, most of them about the building rather than the unit.

Florida's post-Surfside framework is the starting point. Under [section 553.899 of the Florida Statutes](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/553.899), a condominium or cooperative building three habitable stories or more in height needs a milestone structural inspection by December 31 of the year it reaches 30 years of age, and every 10 years after that; local officials may require the first one at 25 years. Under [section 718.112](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/718.112), the association must also complete a structural integrity reserve study, or SIRS, at least every 10 years for each such building, and owners may not vote to underfund the reserves the study requires. Reserves can be funded through regular or special assessments, lines of credit or loans.

That framework creates three points of buyer friction:

- **Assessments and higher monthly dues.** Repairs that were deferred for years are now being funded, which raises the monthly cost of ownership in many older buildings and, in some, adds a special assessment.
- **Conventional financing.** The [Fannie Mae Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-2.1-03/ineligible-projects) treats projects in need of critical repairs, projects that failed a mandatory jurisdictional inspection, and projects whose association is party to pending litigation over safety or structural soundness as ineligible. When a building is ineligible, buyers who depend on a conventional mortgage drop out of the pool.
- **Documentation.** Buildings that have completed their milestone inspection, published their SIRS and funded reserves give buyers a defined cost. Buildings with open questions give buyers a reason to discount.

The buyers who remain are often cash buyers. In October, cash purchases accounted for 48.4% of Miami-Dade condo sales and 52.7% of Broward condo sales, compared with 25.8% and 25.4% of single-family sales. For a condo seller, reaching those buyers, locally and internationally, is not a refinement. It is the core of the plan. Broward condos sold for a median 92% of original list price in October, which shows how much negotiating room buyers currently hold. For how association finances move a sale price, see [HOA Financials and Your Home's Sale Price](/journal/hoa-impact-home-sale-south-florida-2026).

## Fifty Years of Repeat-Sales Data: The Equity Context

Monthly medians move when the mix of sales changes. A month with more waterfront closings lifts the median without any home becoming more valuable. The Federal Housing Finance Agency's all-transactions index avoids that distortion by tracking repeat sales of the same properties. Values from the [FHFA series published on FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ATNHPIUS33124Q) show the long-run picture:

| FHFA all-transactions index | Period | Multiple | Total change | Compound annual rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Miami-Miami Beach-Kendall metropolitan division | Q4 1975 to Q2 2026 | 18.07x | +1,706.92% | 5.90% |
| Florida | Q4 1975 to Q2 2026 | 12.12x | +1,111.77% | 5.06% |
| United States | Q4 1975 to Q2 2026 | 11.55x | +1,055.30% | 4.96% |
| Miami-Dade County (annual series) | 1975 to 2025 | 16.76x | | |
| Palm Beach County (annual series) | 1975 to 2025 | 10.48x | | |
| Broward County (annual series) | 1975 to 2025 | 9.77x | | |

The index covers houses and condos together and measures price change, not the return after costs. Its use for a seller is perspective: an owner of long standing, of either property type, is usually negotiating over the size of the gain, not over a loss. *Long-run appreciation sets the equity. The current segment decides how much of it the sale captures.*

## Should You Sell Your South Florida Home Now?

The data does not answer that question for any single property, but it frames it:

- **Single-family owners** are selling into short supply. The risk is overpricing, not a lack of buyers.
- **Condo owners in buildings with completed inspections and funded reserves** can present a defined cost and compete on documentation.
- **Condo owners in buildings with work still ahead** should understand what the inspection and the reserve study are likely to require before they price, because buyers will.

Personal timing, tax position and the next purchase matter as much as the market. Those questions belong with your tax adviser and attorney; the market side belongs in a pricing analysis built on your building or street, not on a county median.
